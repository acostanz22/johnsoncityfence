What fence work looks like here

Low yards near the Watauga and Doe rivers can hold water after a hard rain. We do not set posts in a saturated hole. If a corner of the lot stays soft, the line moves onto firmer ground or we wait. Hillside streets are the opposite problem: shallow soil over rock, and a grade that has to be stepped. A privacy fence racked like a flat Johnson City backyard will gap at the bottom. Carter County and the city of Elizabethton are not Washington County, so we do not copy a Johnson City permit note onto these parcels. Confirm the rule with the office that governs the lot.

Wet bottoms

River-bottom clay stays soft. Posts go in only where the hole will hold concrete.

Rocky slopes

Hill lots need a stepped layout and a plan for holes that hit rock.

Different county

Elizabethton rules are not the Johnson City 7-foot note. We check the parcel.

Can you install a fence in a yard that floods near the river?

Not in standing water. We look at the low spots first. The line can often move a few feet onto higher ground. If it cannot, we wait or we change the plan.

What fence works on a steep Elizabethton lot?

Panels are stepped so the bottom stays near the grade. Aluminum can rack on a milder slope. A single straight privacy panel pulled downhill opens a gap.

Are Elizabethton fences under Johnson City rules?

No. Elizabethton is in Carter County and has its own city government. We confirm height and permit questions there instead of reusing the Johnson City standard.
